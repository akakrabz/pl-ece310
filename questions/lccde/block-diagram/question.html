Label the signal on every wire (after each delay: $x[n-1]$, $y[n-1]$, …), then write each adder's output as the sum of (gain $\times$ signal) over its inputs. With the feedback gains $g_i$ and the feed-forward gains $b_j$ as drawn, the adders that produce $y[n]$ give the recursion $y[n] = \sum_i g_i\,y[n-i] + \sum_j b_j\,x[n-j]$. Moving $g_i\,y[n-i]$ to the left side gives $a_i = -g_i$: the feedback gains change sign, the feed-forward gains do not. A feedback loop usually makes the impulse response infinite, but not always: check whether $A(z)$ divides $B(z)$.
