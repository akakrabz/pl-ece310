A causal LTI system is described by the difference equation $$ {{params.eq_tex}} $$ and is at initial rest (zero initial conditions: $x[n] = y[n] = 0$ for $n \lt 0$).

(a) Compute the first four samples of the impulse response $h[n]$. Enter exact values: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits.

(b) Is the impulse response of this system finite (FIR) or infinite (IIR)?

{{#params.firiir_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.firiir_choices}} The impulse response is the output for $x[n] = \delta[n]$. Replace every $x[n-k]$ by $\delta[n-k]$ and every $y[n-k]$ by $h[n-k]$, solve for the newest sample $h[n]$, and run it for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$ starting from $h[-1] = h[-2] = 0$. A term $a_k\,y[n-k]$ on the left becomes $-a_k\,h[n-k]$ when you solve for $h[n]$. FIR means $h[n]$ is zero after finitely many samples. A feedback term alone does not decide it: write $H(z)$ and check whether every pole away from $z = 0$ is cancelled by a zero (Lecture 9), or keep running the recursion until the input terms are over and see whether $h[n]$ dies out exactly.

{{#params.has_feedback}}In the Lecture 9 form the equation reads $ {{params.std_tex}} $; solved for the newest output it is the recursion $$ {{params.rec_tex}} . $${{/params.has_feedback}} With $x[n] = \delta[n]$ the output is $h[n]$, and initial rest gives $h[n] = 0$ for $n \lt 0$: $$ {{params.hrec_tex}} .$$ Since $\delta[n-k]$ is $1$ only at $n = k$, at time $n$ the input terms contribute just the coefficient of $x[n-k]$ with $k = n$ (and nothing once $n$ is past the last input term): $$ {{params.steps_tex}} $$ so $h[n] = {{params.h_seq_tex}}$.

{{#params.fir_plain}}

FIR: there are no feedback terms, so $h[n]$ is the list of input coefficients and is zero for $n \gt {{params.nb_last}}$ (Lecture 5 §1.1). Equivalently $H(z) = {{params.H_tex}}$ has no poles away from $z = 0$.

{{/params.fir_plain}} {{#params.fir_rec}}

FIR, despite the feedback term. At $n = {{params.nz}}$ the last input term exactly cancels the fed-back value, $ {{params.hL_tex}} $, and from then on there is no input and $h[n] = {{params.pole_tex}}\,h[n-1] = 0$: the impulse response is finite. In the $z$-domain $$ H(z) = {{params.H_tex}} = {{params.Hnumfact_tex}}, $$ and since ${{params.factor_tex}}$, the pole at $z = {{params.pole_tex}}$ is cancelled by a zero: $$ H(z) = {{params.Hpoly_tex}} , $$ which has no poles away from $z = 0$. A system is IIR only if it keeps a finite pole that no zero cancels (Lecture 9 §1.2, Figure 1). This is the trick of Lecture 5 Eq. (4), the moving average computed recursively, $y[n] = y[n-1] + \tfrac{1}{L}\big(x[n] - x[n-L]\big)$, whose impulse response is finite although the equation has a feedback term.

{{/params.fir_rec}} {{#params.iir}}

IIR: the feedback terms keep the response going after the input has ended (Lecture 5 §1.2). In Lecture 9 terms, $H(z) = {{params.H_tex}}$ has poles away from $z = 0$, and the numerator and denominator share no factor, so no pole is cancelled and $h[n]$ is infinitely long (Lecture 9 §1.2, Figure 1).
