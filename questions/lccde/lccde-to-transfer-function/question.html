A causal LTI system has transfer function $$ H(z) = {{params.Hgiven_tex}} . $$ Write its difference equation in the Lecture 9 form $$ y[n] + \sum_{k=1}^{N} a_k\, y[n-k] = \sum_{k=0}^{M} b_k\, x[n-k], $$ normalised so that the coefficient of $y[n]$ is $1$ (the convention of scipy.signal.lfilter(b, a, x)).

Enter the coefficient vectors as row vectors of decimals with at least 4 significant digits (fractions are not accepted in these boxes), for example [1, -0.75, 0.125]. The vector $b$ starts with $b_0$, the coefficient of $x[n]$, even if it is zero; $a$ starts with $a_0 = 1$. Each vector ends with its last nonzero coefficient.

{{/params.to_ba}} Put every $y$ term on the left and every $x$ term on the right, transform term by term ($y[n-k] \to z^{-k}Y(z)$, $x[n-k] \to z^{-k}X(z)$) and divide: $H(z) = \dfrac{\sum_k b_k z^{-k}}{1 + \sum_k a_k z^{-k}}$. Going the other way, cross-multiply $Y(z)\,(1 + \sum_k a_k z^{-k}) = X(z) \sum_k b_k z^{-k}$ and read off the delays. A term $+c\,y[n-1]$ on the right of a recursion becomes $a_1 = -c$. Before reading off coefficients, write $H$ in powers of $z^{-1}$ (divide numerator and denominator by the highest power of $z$) and divide everything by the constant term of the denominator so that it is $1$. {{#params.to_H}}
