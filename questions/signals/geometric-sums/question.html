Geometric sums are the algebra behind every z-transform. Answer all three parts.

(a) Evaluate $$S = {{params.sumA_tex}}.$$ Enter the exact value: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits.

(b) Find a closed form (no $\sum$, no "$\dots$") of $$s[n] = {{params.sumB_tex}},\qquad {{params.sumB_valid_tex}}.$$ Enter an expression in $n$, for example (2/3)*n^2 + 5^(n-1) (powers with ^; use fractions, not decimals).

(c) For which real or complex $a$ does the series $${{params.conv_tex}}$$ converge?

{{#params.conv_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.conv_choices}} $\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} a^k = \dfrac{1 - a^N}{1 - a}$ for $a \ne 1$ (it is $N$ when $a = 1$), and $\sum_{k=0}^{\infty} a^k = \dfrac{1}{1 - a}$ exactly when $\lvert a\rvert \lt 1$. Any other sum: pull out its first term so that the remaining sum starts at $0$, or substitute $m = -n$ when it runs to $-\infty$. "First term minus first omitted term, over one minus the ratio": $\sum_{k=k_1}^{k_2} a^k = \dfrac{a^{k_1} - a^{k_2+1}}{1 - a}$. A two-sided sum splits at $n = 0$: count the $n = 0$ term exactly as often as the signal does. A sum of roots of unity $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} w^n$ with $w^N = 1$ is $0$ unless $w = 1$. A series that runs to $\pm\infty$ in both directions needs $\lvert\text{ratio}\rvert \lt 1$ on one side and $\gt 1$ on the other.
