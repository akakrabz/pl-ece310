A causal LTI system has the transfer function $$H(z) = {{params.H_tex}},\qquad {{params.roc_tex}}.$$ Its poles are a complex-conjugate pair.

(a) The first samples of the impulse response. Enter exact values: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits.

(b) A closed form of $h[n]$ for $n \ge 0$, as an expression in $n$, in the format of (1/2)^n*(3*cos(pi*n/4) - 2*sqrt(2)*sin(pi*n/4)) (you may use cos, sin, pi, sqrt; angles in radians; use fractions, not decimals):

Write the denominator as $1 - 2r\cos\theta\,z^{-1} + r^2 z^{-2}$ (poles $r e^{\pm j\theta}$) and use $$r^n\cos(\theta n)u[n] \leftrightarrow \dfrac{1 - r\cos\theta\,z^{-1}}{1 - 2r\cos\theta\,z^{-1} + r^2z^{-2}},\qquad r^n\sin(\theta n)u[n] \leftrightarrow \dfrac{r\sin\theta\,z^{-1}}{1 - 2r\cos\theta\,z^{-1} + r^2z^{-2}}.$$ Cross-multiply $H(z)$: ${{params.lfilt_tex}}$, run forward from rest. Your closed form must reproduce these samples.

Poles. ${{params.den_tex}} = 1 - 2r\cos\theta\,z^{-1} + r^2 z^{-2}$ with $r^2 = {{params.r2_tex}}$, so $r = {{params.r_tex}}$, and $2r\cos\theta = {{params.twor_cos_tex}}$ gives $\cos\theta = {{params.cos_th_tex}}$, $\theta = {{params.th_tex}}$: the poles are ${{params.r_tex}}\,e^{\pm j{{params.th_tex}}}$ (inside the ROC ${{params.roc_tex}}$).

Match the numerator. With $r\cos\theta = {{params.rcos_tex}}$ and $r\sin\theta = {{params.rsin_tex}}$, $$A\left(1 - r\cos\theta\,z^{-1}\right) + B\,r\sin\theta\,z^{-1} = {{params.b_tex_num}}.$$ Constant terms: $A = {{params.A_tex}}$. Terms in $z^{-1}$: $B\,r\sin\theta = {{params.b1_tex}} + A\,r\cos\theta = {{params.Brsin_tex}}$, so $B = {{params.B_tex}}$. Hence $$h[n] = {{params.closed_tex}}\,u[n].$$

Samples. $h[0] = {{params.h0_tex}}$, $h[1] = {{params.h1_tex}}$, $h[2] = {{params.h2_tex}}$; the recursion ${{params.lfilt_tex}}$ gives the same: $h[0] = {{params.h0_tex}}$, ${{params.rec1_tex}}$, ${{params.rec2_tex}}$.
