(c) Find the impulse response $h[n]$ of the system: the index of its first nonzero sample, and its samples from the first to the last nonzero one as a row vector, zeros in between included (for example [4, 0, -1, 2]).

(d) The output is $y[n] = x[n] * h[n]$, so $Y(z) = X(z)\,H(z)$. Find the output sample $y[{{params.k_tex}}]$, which is the coefficient of ${{params.zk_tex}}$ in $Y(z)$.

(e) Evaluate $Y(z)$ at $z = {{params.z0_tex}}$.

$\delta[n-k] \leftrightarrow z^{-k}$, so a finite sequence transforms term by term: the sample $x[k]$ becomes $x[k]\,z^{-k}$. A sample at a negative index $k$ gives a positive power $z^{\lvert k\rvert}$. Read $H(z)$ backwards the same way: the coefficient of $z^{-n}$ is $h[n]$. Negative powers of $z$ blow up at $z = 0$; positive powers blow up as $z \to \infty$. In $Y(z) = X(z)H(z)$ the term $z^{-k}$ collects every product $x[m]z^{-m}\cdot h[k-m]z^{-(k-m)}$, which is the convolution sum. At $z = 1$ every power of $z$ equals $1$, so $Y(1) = X(1)\,H(1) = \sum_n x[n]\cdot\sum_n h[n]$; at $z = -1$ each sample picks up the sign $(-1)^n$.
