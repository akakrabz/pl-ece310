A factor that appears in both numerator and denominator cancels, and a cancelled pole does not bound an ROC. Test each denominator factor $1 - p z^{-1}$: does the numerator vanish at $z^{-1} = 1/p$? Then the ROCs are the regions between consecutive distinct pole magnitudes: $k$ magnitudes give $k + 1$ ROCs. Cover-up: $A_k = \big[(1 - p_k z^{-1})H(z)\big]_{z = p_k}$. In a given ROC, a pole inside the inner circle gives $A_k\,p_k^n u[n]$ (only $n \ge 0$) and a pole outside the outer circle gives $-A_k\,p_k^n u[-n-1]$ (only $n \le -1$). BIBO stable $\Leftrightarrow$ the ROC contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$. {{#params.cancel}}
