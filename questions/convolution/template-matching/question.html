The normalized score divides it by the largest value the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality allows for the input samples under the template: $$\rho[n] = \frac{y[n]}{\sqrt{\sum_{m} t[m]^2}\;\sqrt{\sum_{k = {{params.win_lo_tex}} }^{ {{params.win_hi_tex}} } x[k]^2}}, \qquad -1 \le \rho[n] \le 1.$$ Find the normalized score at the peak. Enter a decimal with 4 significant digits (or an exact fraction if it is one).

The matched filter is the time-reversed template, $h[n] = t[-n]$: the sample of $t$ at index $m$ moves to index $-m$. Then $y[n] = \sum_k x[k]\,h[n-k] = \sum_k x[k]\,t[k-n]$: the template itself, moved so that its sample $t[0]$ sits at $k = n$, multiplied sample by sample with $x$ and added. $y[n]$ is largest where the input window under the shifted template looks most like the template. If the copy in $x$ is $c\,t[n - n_0]$, the peak is at $n = n_0$ (not at the end of the copy). For $\rho$, use the three input samples under the template at the peak.
