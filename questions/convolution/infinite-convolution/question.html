{{#params.is_exp}} Write $y[n] = \sum_k x[k]\,h[n-k]$ with both unit steps kept. A factor $u[k - a]$ means $k \ge a$, a factor $u[n - k - b]$ means $k \le n - b$. The sum is empty (so $y[n] = 0$) unless the lower limit is at most the upper limit. Pull every factor that depends only on $n$ out of the sum. Then $\sum_{k=K_1}^{K_2} r^k = \dfrac{r^{K_1} - r^{K_2+1}}{1-r}$ for $r \ne 1$, and $\sum_{k=K_1}^{K_2} 1 = K_2 - K_1 + 1$. Check your formula at $n = n_0$: it must give $y[n_0] = x[\text{first}]\,h[\text{first}]$. {{/params.is_exp}} {{#params.is_finite}} $x[n] = \sum_m c_m\,\delta[n - m]$, so $y[n] = \sum_m c_m\,h[n - m]$: a sum of scaled, shifted copies of $h$. Shift each copy everywhere, step included. The copy $c_m\,h[n-m]$ starts where its step switches on. Once $n$ is past the start of the last copy, all steps equal 1 and the copies combine into one multiple of the same exponential. {{/params.is_finite}} {{#params.is_ab}}

Convolution sum with the steps kept: $$y[n] = {{params.sum_tex}}.$$ The steps restrict the sum to ${{params.range_tex}}$, which is non-empty only for $n \ge {{params.k1}} + ({{params.k2}})$. Hence $y[n] = 0$ for $n \lt n_0$ and $\boxed{n_0 = {{params.n_first}}}$, with $y[n_0] = {{params.y0_tex}}$.

For $n \ge n_0$, pull out the factors that depend only on $n$ and sum the geometric series whose ratio is (base of $x$)/(base of $h$) $= r = {{params.r_tex}}$: $$y[n] = {{params.geo_tex}},$$ which simplifies to $$\boxed{\,y[n] = {{params.y_tex}}\,}\qquad (n \ge {{params.n_first}}),$$ i.e. $y[n] = \big({{params.y_tex}}\big)\,u[n-n_0]$. Check at $n = n_0$: the formula gives the value $x[\text{first}]\,h[\text{first}]$ found above.
