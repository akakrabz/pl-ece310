{{/params.is_exp}} {{#params.is_fir}} $s[n] = \sum_k h[k]\,u[n-k] = \sum_{k \le n} h[k]$: the running sum of $h$. It is $0$ before the first sample of $h$. After the last nonzero sample of $h$ nothing more is added, so $s[n]$ stays at $\sum_n h[n]$ (the DC gain $H(1)$). {{/params.is_fir}} {{#params.is_exp}} $\delta[n] = u[n] - u[n-1]$, so by linearity and time invariance $h[n] = s[n] - s[n-1]$. At $n = 0$ the delayed term is $s[-1] = 0$ (because of the $u[n]$), so $h[0] = s[0]$. For $n \ge 1$ both terms use the formula; the constant cancels and $a^n - a^{n-1} = a^{n-1}(a-1)$. {{/params.is_exp}} {{#params.is_fir}}

$s[n] = h[n] * u[n] = \sum_k h[k]\,u[n-k]$: one shifted step per sample of $h$, $$s[n] = {{params.steps_tex}},$$ i.e. the running sum $s[n] = \sum_{k \le n} h[k]$:

{{{params.table_html}}}

(a) $\big[\,s[{{params.w0}}], \dots, s[{{params.w_last}}]\,\big]$ is read off the table ($s = 0$ before $h$ starts). (b) From $n = {{params.n_last}}$ on, nothing more is added: $s[n] = \sum_n h[n] = {{params.sum_tex}}$.

Modelled on FA2019 #3(b) (step response of a finite $h$ as a sum of shifted steps), HW2 #3 and Lecture 4 (convolution with $u[n]$ is a running sum).

{{/params.is_fir}} {{#params.is_exp}}

Since $\delta[n] = u[n] - u[n-1]$, linearity and time invariance give $h[n] = s[n] - s[n-1]$.

$n = 0$: $s[-1] = 0$, so $h[0] = s[0] = {{params.s0_tex}} = {{params.h0_tex}}$.

$n \ge 1$: both $s[n]$ and $s[n-1]$ use the formula $s[n] = {{params.s_inner_tex}}$, and the constant cancels: $$h[n] = {{params.diff_tex}} = {{params.fact_tex}} = {{params.hn_tex}},\qquad n \ge 1.$$

Altogether $$h[n] = {{params.h_full_tex}}.$$ {{#params.has_delta}}(The formula for $n \ge 1$ does not hold at $n = 0$; the difference is the $\delta[n]$ term.){{/params.has_delta}}

Modelled on FA2023 #4 ($h[n] = s[n] - s[n-1]$ from a given step response) and HW2 #3 ($\delta[n] = u[n] - u[n-1]$); see also Lecture 4 §2.2.3, where $u[n] * \left(-\tfrac34\right)^n u[n]$ is a step response.
