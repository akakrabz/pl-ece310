{{#params.rate_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.rate_choices}} A sinusoid at $\Omega_0$ rad/s is a pair of impulses at $\pm\Omega_0$, i.e. $f_0 = \tfrac{\Omega_0}{2\pi}$ Hz. $\tfrac{\sin(\Omega_c t)}{\pi t}$ is a box on $\lvert\Omega\rvert \lt \Omega_c$. Products in time convolve spectra (band edges add), convolutions in time multiply spectra (the smaller edge wins), $x(at)$ scales every frequency by $a$, and multiplying by $\cos(\Omega_0 t)$ shifts the spectrum by $\pm\Omega_0$. Lecture 17: the copies of the spectrum do not overlap when $f_s \gt 2B$. The Nyquist rate is $2B$ in hertz (not in rad/s), and $T_N = \tfrac{1}{2B}$. The inequality is strict: sampling exactly at $2B$ is not enough (a sine at $B$ sampled at $2B$ gives $\sin(\pi n) = 0$).
