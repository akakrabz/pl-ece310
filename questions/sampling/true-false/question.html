Analog frequencies $\Omega$ are in rad/s, $f$ and $f_s$ in Hz; the $N$-point DFT is $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$.

{{#choices}}{{text}}{{/choices}} {{/params.statements}} Sampling with period $T$ maps $\Omega$ to $\omega = \Omega T$ (modulo $2\pi$), copies the spectrum every $2\pi$ and scales it by $1/T$; no overlap needs $f_s \gt 2B$. An ideal D/A turns each sample into a sinc through the sample points; a zero-order hold makes a staircase. $X[k] = X_d\left(\tfrac{2\pi k}{N}\right)$; the inverse-DFT formula is $N$-periodic in $n$; zero padding samples the same DTFT more densely. Multiplying $N$-point DFTs gives an $N$-point circular convolution. Write out the samples: $\cos(\Omega_0 nT)$ for a few $n$, or a sinc sum at one $t$. For DFT claims try $x[n] = \delta[n-1]$, whose DFT is $e^{-j2\pi k/N}$, or count the periods of a cosine in the record (a whole number means it sits on DFT bins). For an aliased tone, fold its frequency into $[0, f_s/2]$ before it goes anywhere else.
