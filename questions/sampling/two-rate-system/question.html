(c) Now the two converters share one period, $T_1 = T_2 = T$, and the output is $${{params.yc_tex}}.$$ Find every sampling period $T$ for which this happens. Enter the largest such $T$ and how many such $T$ there are.

The A/D maps $\Omega$ to $\omega = \Omega T_1$; reduce $\omega$ by multiples of $2\pi$ to $(-\pi, \pi]$, then use $\sin(-\theta) = -\sin\theta$ and $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$ to make the frequency non-negative. The ideal D/A maps $\omega$ back to $\Omega' = \omega / T_2$ (its own period, not $T_1$). A sine sampled at a multiple of $\pi$ gives $\sin(k\pi n) = 0$ for every $n$; a cosine sampled at a multiple of $2\pi$ gives the constant $\cos(2\pi k n) = 1$. A term that must come out unchanged must not alias: $\Omega T \lt \pi$. A term that must come out at another frequency is an alias: $\Omega T = \pm\Omega_{\text{out}} T + 2\pi k$, and for a sine only the $+$ keeps its sign. {{#params.named}}
