A continuous-time signal $x_a(t)$ is processed by an ideal A/D converter with sampling period $T$ ($x[n] = x_a(nT)$), a digital filter $H_d(\omega)$, and an ideal D/A converter with the same $T$:

{{{params.chain_html}}} {{#params.is_analysis}}

The input is $$x_a(t) = {{params.an.x_tex}},$$ with $t$ in seconds; the sampling period is ${{params.an.T_tex}}$ ($f_s = {{params.an.fs_tex}}$ Hz), and $H_d(\omega)$ is the ideal {{params.an.kind_word}} filter (one period shown; $H_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic) $${{params.an.hd_tex}}$$

{{{params.an.hd_svg_html}}} {{/params.is_analysis}} {{#params.is_design}}

The input $x_a(t)$ is band-limited to $B = {{params.de.B_tex}}$: $X_a(\Omega) = 0$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert \ge {{params.de.OmB_tex}}$ rad/s. {{#params.de.is_lpf}}We want the whole system to act as an ideal analog low-pass filter with cutoff $F_c = {{params.de.Fc_tex}}$: $y_a(t)$ must contain every component of $x_a(t)$ with $\lvert f\rvert \lt F_c$, unchanged, and nothing of $x_a(t)$ above $F_c$. $H_d(\omega)$ is an ideal digital low-pass filter: $1$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert \le \omega_c$, $0$ for $\omega_c \lt \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi$. Some aliasing is allowed, as long as the output is still exactly this.{{/params.de.is_lpf}} {{#params.de.is_hpf}}We want the whole system to act as an ideal analog high-pass filter with cutoff $F_c = {{params.de.Fc_tex}}$: $y_a(t)$ must contain every component of $x_a(t)$ with $F_c \lt \lvert f\rvert \lt B$, unchanged, and nothing of $x_a(t)$ below $F_c$. $H_d(\omega)$ is an ideal digital high-pass filter: $0$ for $\lvert\omega\rvert \lt \omega_c$, $1$ for $\omega_c \le \lvert\omega\rvert \le \pi$.{{/params.de.is_hpf}}

{{/params.is_design}}

Enter exact values: fractions such as 3/7 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits.

The output $y_a(t)$ is a sum of cosines. For each frequency below, enter the amplitude of the cosine at that frequency in $y_a(t)$ (enter 0 if $y_a(t)$ has no component there).

(a) Find the largest sampling period $T$ (in seconds) for which the system can do this, and the cutoff $\omega_c$ of $H_d(\omega)$ at that $T$, as a multiple of $\pi$.

(b) A different input is sampled fast enough that no aliasing occurs, with ${{params.de.T2_tex}}$, and $H_d(\omega)$ is an ideal digital {{params.de.kind_word}} filter with cutoff $\omega_c = {{params.de.wc2_tex}}$. The whole system then acts as an ideal analog {{params.de.kind_word}} filter. What is its cutoff frequency in hertz?

{{/params.is_design}} The sampler maps $f$ Hz to the digital frequency $\omega = 2\pi f T$ and repeats the spectrum every $2\pi$ (height $\tfrac1T$). $H_d$ acts on $\omega$ reduced to $(-\pi, \pi]$. The ideal D/A maps $\omega$ back to $\omega/(2\pi T)$ Hz, keeps only $\lvert f\rvert \lt \tfrac{1}{2T}$, and its gain $T$ cancels the sampler's $\tfrac1T$. {{#params.is_analysis}} For each cosine: $\omega = 2\pi f/f_s$; subtract the nearest multiple of $2\pi$ and take $\lvert\omega\rvert$ (a cosine is even). If $H_d(\lvert\omega\rvert) = 1$ the tone comes out at $\lvert\omega\rvert f_s/(2\pi)$ Hz with its amplitude; tones that land on the same $\lvert\omega\rvert$ add. Nothing comes out at or above $f_s/2$. {{/params.is_analysis}} {{#params.is_design}} Draw the baseband copy on $\lvert\omega\rvert \le 2\pi BT$ and the copy centred at $2\pi$, which starts at $2\pi - 2\pi BT$. Overlap is harmless only where $H_d$ deletes it, and the band you keep must also come back through the D/A ($\lvert f\rvert \lt \tfrac{1}{2T}$). Without aliasing the system is LTI with $H_{\text{eff}}(\Omega) = H_d(\Omega T)$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert \lt \pi/T$. {{/params.is_design}} {{#params.is_analysis}}

Each tone $A\cos(2\pi f t)$ gives the samples $A\cos(\omega n)$ with $\omega = 2\pi f/f_s = 2\pi f T$. Reduce $\omega$ to its principal value (subtract multiples of $2\pi$; $\cos(-\theta) = \cos\theta$, so only $\lvert\omega\rvert$ matters for these zero-phase cosines). $H_d$ keeps the tone if $H_d(\lvert\omega\rvert) = 1$, and the ideal D/A rebuilds it at $\lvert\omega\rvert f_s/(2\pi)$ Hz with the same amplitude (the D/A's gain $T$ cancels the sampler's $\tfrac1T$). Only frequencies below $f_s/2 = {{params.an.half_tex}}$ Hz can come out.

{{/params.is_analysis}} {{#params.is_design}} {{#params.de.is_lpf}}

(a) Keeping $\lvert f\rvert \lt F_c$ means a digital cutoff $\omega_c = 2\pi F_c T$. The band of $x_a$ ends at $\omega = 2\pi BT$, so the spectral copy centred at $2\pi$ starts at $2\pi - 2\pi BT$. Aliasing is harmless as long as that copy stays out of the passband: $$2\pi F_c T \le 2\pi - 2\pi B T \iff T \le \frac{1}{B + F_c} = \frac{1}{ {{params.de.BplusFc_tex}} }\ \text{s}.$$ So $T_{\max} = {{params.de.Tmax_tex}}$ s and $\omega_c = 2\pi F_c T_{\max} = {{params.de.wc_tex}}$. The D/A at this $T$ rebuilds $\lvert f\rvert \lt \tfrac{1}{2T}$, which includes the passband. The Nyquist period $\tfrac{1}{2B} = {{params.de.Tnyq_tex}}$ s also works, but it is not the largest: the aliased parts land where $H_d$ deletes them anyway (SP2025 #5(b), FA2024 #5(c), FA2023 #5(a)).

{{/params.de.is_lpf}} {{#params.de.is_hpf}}

(a) The band to keep, $F_c \lt \lvert f\rvert \lt B$, reaches up to $B$, so the D/A must be able to rebuild $B$: $\tfrac{1}{2T} \ge B$. Equivalently, the copy centred at $2\pi$, which starts at $2\pi - 2\pi BT$, would otherwise overlap the top of the band, exactly where a high-pass filter keeps everything. No aliasing can be allowed: $$T \le \frac{1}{2B} = \frac{1}{ {{params.de.twoB_tex}} }\ \text{s},$$ so $T_{\max} = {{params.de.Tmax_tex}}$ s and $\omega_c = 2\pi F_c T_{\max} = {{params.de.wc_tex}}$ (FA2023 #5(b); the low-pass trick $T = \tfrac{1}{B + F_c}$ does not work here).

{{/params.de.is_hpf}}

(b) Without aliasing the whole system is LTI with $H_{\text{eff}}(\Omega) = H_d(\Omega T)$ for $\lvert\Omega\rvert \lt \pi/T$ (2019 notes §5.3; FA2019 #9(b)), so the digital cutoff maps to $\Omega_c = \omega_c/T = {{params.de.wc2_tex}}\cdot{{params.de.fs2_tex}} = {{params.de.Omc2_tex}}$ rad/s, i.e. $$f_c = \frac{\omega_c}{2\pi T} = {{params.de.fc2_tex}}\ \text{Hz}.$$
