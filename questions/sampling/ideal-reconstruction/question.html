Ahead of the lectures: this topic is taught after Lecture 17 and appears on past Midterm 2 exams; notation follows those exams.

An ideal D/A converter with sampling period $T$ turns a sequence $x[n]$ into $$y_a(t) = \sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty} x[n]\,\frac{\sin\big(\pi(t - nT)/T\big)}{\pi(t - nT)/T}$$ (each ratio equals $1$ at $t = nT$). A zero-order hold (ZOH) with period $T$ holds each sample instead: its output is $y_{\text{ZOH}}(t) = x[n]$ for $nT \le t \lt (n+1)T$.

Enter exact values where you can: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits (for example 0.5774 for $1/\sqrt{3}$).

(a) The sequence $x[n] = {{params.a.x_tex}}$, defined for all $n$, is applied to an ideal D/A converter with ${{params.a.T_tex}}$. The output can be written $y_a(t) = C\cos(2\pi f_0 t + \phi)$ with $C \gt 0$, $f_0 \ge 0$ and $-\pi \lt \phi \le \pi$. Find $f_0$ in hertz, $C$, and $\phi$ as a multiple of $\pi$ (for $\phi = -\tfrac{2\pi}{7}$ you would type -2/7).

(b) Now take {{{params.b.x_intro_html}}} and ${{params.b.Tb_tex}}$. Find the ZOH output at ${{params.b.t1_tex}}$, and the ideal D/A output at ${{params.b.t2_tex}}$ and at ${{params.b.t3_tex}}$.

(c) An analog signal $x_a(t)$ is sampled by an ideal A/D converter at $f_s = {{params.c.fsc_tex}}$ Hz, and the samples $x[n] = x_a(nT)$ go straight into an ideal D/A converter with the same $T$. The output is $y_a(t) = {{params.c.yc_tex}}$ ($t$ in seconds, frequencies in rad/s). Which of these inputs could have produced this output? Select all that apply.

{{#params.inputs_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.inputs_choices}} The ideal D/A is an ideal low-pass interpolator: it rebuilds the one signal with frequencies below $\frac{\pi}{T}$ rad/s ($\frac{f_s}{2}$ Hz) that passes through the samples. So reduce the digital frequency to $(-\pi, \pi]$ first (subtract multiples of $2\pi$; use $\cos(-u) = \cos u$), then replace $n$ by $t/T$: $\omega_0$ rad/sample becomes $\omega_0/T$ rad/s, i.e. $\omega_0/(2\pi T)$ Hz. At $t = mT$ every term except $n = m$ vanishes, so $y_a(mT) = x[m]$. Halfway, at $t = (m + \tfrac12)T$, each $\sin\big(\pi(t - nT)/T\big) = \pm 1$, so sample $n$ contributes $\pm\dfrac{x[n]}{\pi\,(m - n + \frac12)}$: the two nearest samples get $\frac{2}{\pi}$, the next two $-\frac{2}{3\pi}$, and so on. The ZOH just holds the most recent sample. In (c), an input at $F$ Hz lands at $\omega = 2\pi F/f_s$; it gives the same samples only if $\omega$ reduces to $+\omega_0$ with the same phase, or to $-\omega_0$ with the opposite phase.
