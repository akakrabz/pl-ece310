{{#choices}}{{text}}{{/choices}} {{/params.statements}} Most statements with "always", "never", "must" or "cannot" are False and die to one counterexample: $h = u[n]$ (bounded but unstable), $h = \delta[n] - \delta[n-1]$ (undoes $u[n]$), $h = (\tfrac12)^{\lvert n\rvert}$ (two-sided and stable), $y = n\,x[n]$ (causal, time-varying, $h = 0$), $y = x^2[n]$ (nonlinear with $h = \delta[n]$). A stable LTI system's ROC contains $\lvert z\rvert = 1$, and that decides the sidedness: poles inside the unit circle give right-sided terms, poles outside give left-sided terms. If no ROC is given, you may choose it. A pole on the unit circle rules out stability.
