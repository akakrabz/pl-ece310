The phase response $\angle H_d(\omega)$ is reported as a principal value in $(-\pi, \pi]$, the full circle, not the range $(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2})$ of $\tan^{-1}$.

Enter every angle in radians, as the principal value in $(-\pi, \pi]$, with at least 4 significant digits (type 0.1234, not 0.12). An angle off by a multiple of $2\pi$ is marked wrong. On the negative real axis both 3.1416 and -3.1416 are accepted.

(a) At a frequency $\omega_1$ a system has $H_d(\omega_1) = {{params.za_tex}}$. Find its phase.

(b) At another frequency $\omega_2$, $H_d(\omega_2) = {{params.zb_tex}}$. Find its phase.

(c) A second system has $$H_d(\omega) = {{params.lin_tex}}\,A(\omega),\qquad A(\omega) = {{params.A_tex}}\quad(\text{real}).$$ Find the principal phase at the three frequencies below.

$\tan^{-1}(b/a)$ only returns angles in $(-\tfrac{\pi}{2}, \tfrac{\pi}{2})$: it cannot tell $a + jb$ from $-a - jb$. Find the quadrant of $a + jb$ first; if $a \lt 0$, add $\pi$ (when $b \ge 0$) or subtract $\pi$ (when $b \lt 0$). $|e^{-jM\omega}| = 1$, so the phase is $-M\omega$ where $A(\omega) \gt 0$ and $-M\omega \pm \pi$ where $A(\omega) \lt 0$ ($-1 = e^{\pm j\pi}$). Then add or subtract multiples of $2\pi$ until the angle lies in $(-\pi, \pi]$.

Lecture 16, §2–4: principal angle in $(-\pi, \pi]$; a negative real factor adds $\pm\pi$; a linear phase wraps by $2\pi$.
