$H_d(\omega) = \sum_n h[n]\,e^{-j\omega n}$. Factor out $e^{-j\omega M}$, where $M$ is the midpoint of the first and last nonzero samples, and pair the remaining terms: $e^{jk\omega} + e^{-jk\omega} = 2\cos k\omega$, $e^{jk\omega} - e^{-jk\omega} = 2j\sin k\omega$. $|H_d(\omega)| = |A(\omega)|$, never negative. Where $A(\omega) \lt 0$ write $A = |A|\,e^{\pm j\pi}$: the phase is $-\alpha\omega$ ({{#params.is_anti}}$+\tfrac{\pi}{2}$ for the $j$, {{/params.is_anti}}$\pm\pi$ for the sign), then reduce it to $(-\pi, \pi]$. The group delay is minus the slope of the phase between the jumps.

(e) Between its jumps the phase is a line of slope $-\alpha$, so the group delay is uniform: $\tau(\omega) = {{params.alpha_tex}}$ samples. The $\pm\pi$ jumps (sign changes of $A$) and the $2\pi$ wraps do not count.

(f) $|H_d(\omega)| = 0$ where $A(\omega) = 0$. On $(0, \pi]$ that happens at $\omega = {{params.zeros_tex}}${{#params.several_zeros}}; the smallest is ${{params.wzero_tex}}${{/params.several_zeros}}, so the answer is ${{params.wzero_plain}}\cdot\pi$.
