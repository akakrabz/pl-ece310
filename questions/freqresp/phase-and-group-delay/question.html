{{#params.jump_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.jump_choices}} Write $H_d(\omega)$ as a factor of modulus one times a real function $R(\omega)$. Then $|H_d| = |R|$, and the phase is the angle of the unit-modulus factor, plus $\pm\pi$ where $R(\omega) \lt 0$ (because $-1 = e^{\pm j\pi}$). A ratio such as $\dfrac{1}{1 - a e^{-j\omega}}$ has no such factor: compute magnitude and angle of the denominator and invert. A $\pm\pi$ jump happens only where the real factor changes sign (a zero of $|H_d|$). A $2\pi$ wrap happens where the phase reaches $\pm\pi$; it changes nothing. The group delay is minus the slope of the phase between jumps and wraps; for $\dfrac{1}{1 - a e^{-j\omega}}$ it is $\tau(\omega) = \dfrac{a\cos\omega - a^2}{1 - 2a\cos\omega + a^2}$.
