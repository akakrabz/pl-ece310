$e^{j\omega_0 n} \mapsto H_d(\omega_0)\,e^{j\omega_0 n}$, and the coefficient in front stays. A constant is frequency $0$; $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n}$, $j^n = e^{j\frac{\pi}{2}n}$; a cosine or sine is two exponentials, at $+\omega_0$ and $-\omega_0$. Bring every frequency into $[-\pi, \pi]$ first ($e^{j2\pi n} = 1$). $h[n]$ is real exactly when $H_d(-\omega) = H_d^*(\omega)$ (even magnitude, odd phase). Only then does the half at $-\omega_0$ get the conjugate gain. In every case $C_{-}$ uses $H_d(-\omega_0)$, evaluated with $-\omega_0$ plugged in. For the angles: multiply magnitudes, add angles, then reduce to $(-\pi, \pi]$.
