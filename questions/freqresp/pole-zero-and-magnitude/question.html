The pole-zero plots of four causal, stable LTI systems are shown below ($\times$: pole, $\circ$: zero, a small number next to a symbol: its multiplicity; dashed: the unit circle).

On the unit circle $z = e^{j\omega}$, a system $H(z) = K\,\dfrac{\prod_k \left(1 - z_k z^{-1}\right)}{\prod_k \left(1 - p_k z^{-1}\right)}$ has $$|H_d(\omega)| = |K|\,\frac{\prod_k \left|e^{j\omega} - z_k\right|}{\prod_k \left|e^{j\omega} - p_k\right|},$$ the distances from the point $e^{j\omega}$ to the zeros over the distances to the poles. So a pole close to the unit circle at angle $\omega_0$ makes a peak near $\omega = \omega_0$, and a zero on the circle at angle $\omega_0$ makes an exact null ${|H_d(\omega_0)| = 0.}$

The frequency $\omega = 0$ is the point $z = 1$ and $\omega = \pi$ is the point $z = -1$. Look first for zeros on the unit circle (exact nulls), then for poles close to it (peaks). Low-pass: large near $z = 1$, zero at $z = -1$. High-pass: the reverse. Band-pass: zero at both $z = \pm 1$ and a peak in between, where the poles come close to the circle. Notch (band-stop): an exact null at one angle in between, with the gain close to constant elsewhere because a pole sits right next to each zero.
