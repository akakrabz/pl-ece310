By symmetry $\mathbf{H} = \hat{\phi}H_\phi(r)$, so a circle of radius $r$ gives $\oint\mathbf{H}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 2\pi r\,H_\phi = I_{\text{enc}}$. The whole problem is bookkeeping of $I_{\text{enc}}(r)$: inside a uniform conductor the enclosed current scales with the enclosed area. For $b < r < c$ the enclosed current is $I$ from the core minus the fraction $\dfrac{r^2 - b^2}{c^2 - b^2}$ of the return current $I$. It falls from $I$ at $r = b$ to $0$ at $r = c$.

(a) $I_{\text{enc}} = I\,\dfrac{r^2}{a^2}$, so $H_\phi = \dfrac{I r}{2\pi a^2} = {{params.H1_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$ — the field grows linearly inside a uniform conductor.

(b) $H_\phi = \dfrac{I}{2\pi r} = {{params.H2_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$, the same as an infinite line current.

(c) $I_{\text{enc}} = I\left(1 - \dfrac{r^2 - b^2}{c^2 - b^2}\right) = I\,\dfrac{c^2 - r^2}{c^2 - b^2} = {{params.Ienc3_str}}\ \mathrm{A}$, so $H_\phi = \dfrac{I_{\text{enc}}}{2\pi r} = {{params.H3_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$.

(d) $I_{\text{enc}} = I - I = 0$: $H_\phi = 0$. A coax confines its magnetic field, just as it confines its electric field.

(e) With both currents in $+\hat{z}$, $I_{\text{enc}} = 2I$ and $H_\phi = \dfrac{2I}{2\pi r} = {{params.H4same_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$.

(f) Right-hand rule: thumb along $+\hat{z}$ (toward you), fingers curl counterclockwise. Magnetic field lines have no beginning or end — they close on themselves around the current.
