Ampère's law on a rectangle straddling a single sheet $\mathbf{J}_s = J_s\hat{z}$ at $x = 0$ gives $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12 J_s\,\hat{y}\,\mathrm{sgn}(x)$: a uniform field of magnitude $J_s/2$ on either side, reversing across the sheet. In general $\mathbf{H} = \tfrac12\mathbf{J}_s\times\hat{n}$ with $\hat{n}$ the unit normal from the sheet toward the field point. Superpose the two half-fields region by region, minding the sign of each $\mathrm{sgn}(\cdot)$. Sheet 2 feels only the field of sheet 1: $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{J}_{s2}\times\mathbf{B}_1(x = d)$, force per unit area.

Each sheet contributes $\tfrac12 J_s\,\mathrm{sgn}(x - x_{\text{sheet}})\,\hat{y}$:

(a) $x < 0$: both signs negative, $H_y = -\tfrac12({{params.Js1}}) - \tfrac12({{params.Js2}}) = {{params.Hleft_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$.

(b) $0 < x < d$: $H_y = +\tfrac12({{params.Js1}}) - \tfrac12({{params.Js2}}) = {{params.Hmid_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$.

(c) $x > d$: $H_y = +\tfrac12({{params.Js1}}) + \tfrac12({{params.Js2}}) = {{params.Hright_str}}\ \mathrm{A/m}$. (For $\mathbf{J}_{s2} = -\mathbf{J}_{s1}$ the field would be confined between the sheets — the "parallel-plate" inductor of Lecture 15.)

(d) Sheet 1 produces $\mathbf{B}_1 = \tfrac12\mu_0({{params.Js1}})\,\hat{y}$ at $x = d$. Then $\mathbf{f} = \mathbf{J}_{s2}\times\mathbf{B}_1 = ({{params.Js2}})\hat{z}\times\tfrac12\mu_0({{params.Js1}})\hat{y} = -\tfrac12\mu_0({{params.Js1}})({{params.Js2}})\,\hat{x}$, i.e. $f_x = {{params.fx_str}}\ \mu\mathrm{N/m^2}$: {{{params.force_words}}}

(e) $\hat{z}\times\hat{x} = \hat{y}$, so with ${{params.Js1}}\,\hat{z}$ the field at $x > 0$ is along {{{params.dir_correct}}} — the right-hand rule applied to every filament of the sheet.
