{{#params.type_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.type_choices}} $H_d(\omega) = H(z)\big|_{z = e^{j\omega}}$. The points $\omega = 0,\ \tfrac{\pi}{2},\ \pi$ are $z = 1,\ j,\ -1$, where $e^{-jk\omega}$ equals $1$, $(-j)^k$ and $(-1)^k$. For a ratio, $|N/D| = |N|/|D|$. For a real impulse response, $\cos(\omega_0 n + \theta) \mapsto |H_d(\omega_0)|\cos\big(\omega_0 n + \theta + \angle H_d(\omega_0)\big)$: the amplitude is scaled by the gain at $\omega_0$. The three gains at $0$, $\tfrac{\pi}{2}$, $\pi$ also tell you where the filter passes and where it attenuates.

Modelled on Lecture 14 ($H_d(\omega) = H(z)$ on the unit circle, which exists because the system is stable) and the sinusoidal response of an LTI system (course summary; eigenfunctions, Lecture 6); evaluating a transform on the unit circle is the method of HW5 Problem 1(d).
