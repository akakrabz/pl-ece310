(c) Find the frequency $\omega_2$ with $0 \le \omega_2 \le \pi$ such that $\cos(\omega_2 n) = \cos\left({{params.w1_n_tex}}\right)$ for every integer $n$. Enter it as a multiple of $\pi$: for $\omega_2 = \tfrac{2\pi}{7}$ type 2/7 (fractions are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits).

$e^{j\omega n}$ and $\cos(\omega n)$ repeat after $N$ samples exactly when $\omega N$ is a multiple of $2\pi$. Write $\dfrac{\omega}{2\pi} = \dfrac{K}{L}$ in lowest terms: the period is $L$. If $\dfrac{\omega}{2\pi}$ is irrational (no $\pi$ in $\omega$, e.g. $\cos(n/3)$), no $N$ works. A sum of periodic terms repeats after the least common multiple of their periods; for a product, first multiply the exponentials out ($e^{jan}e^{jbn} = e^{j(a+b)n}$, $\cos\theta = \tfrac12(e^{j\theta} + e^{-j\theta})$). Frequencies $2\pi$ apart give identical samples, and $\cos$ is even, so $\cos(\omega n) = \cos((2\pi k \pm \omega)n)$.
