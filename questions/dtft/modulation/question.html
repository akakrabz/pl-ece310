(b) $Y_d(\omega)$ is built from shifted copies of $X_d(\omega)$. Consider the copy whose centre lies in $0 \lt \omega \le \pi$ (not the one centred at $\omega = 0$). Before any folding back by $2\pi$-periodicity it occupies $\omega_L \le \omega \le \omega_H$; $\omega_L$ may be negative and $\omega_H$ may exceed $\pi$. Enter both edges as multiples of $\pi$ (for $\tfrac{2\pi}{7}$ type 2/7).

$e^{j\omega_0 n}x[n] \leftrightarrow X_d(\omega - \omega_0)$. A cosine is two exponentials, so $x[n]\cos(\omega_0 n) \leftrightarrow \tfrac12 X_d(\omega - \omega_0) + \tfrac12 X_d(\omega + \omega_0)$; $\cos^2\theta = \tfrac12 + \tfrac12\cos 2\theta$; and $(-1)^n = e^{j\pi n}$. Use the periodic $X_d$: a copy that runs past $+\pi$ comes back in at $-\pi$ (and vice versa). Where copies overlap, their heights add.
