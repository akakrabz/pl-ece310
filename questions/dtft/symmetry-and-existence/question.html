{{#params.s2_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.s2_choices}} Every DTFT is $2\pi$-periodic: $X_d(\omega + 2\pi) = X_d(\omega)$. Only a real $x[n]$ adds Hermitian symmetry, $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$ (and conversely: Hermitian $X_d$ means real $x$). Symmetry flips the sign of $\omega$ and conjugates; periodicity only shifts the argument. $\sum_n|x[n]| \lt \infty$ $\iff$ the ROC contains $|z| = 1$; then substitute $z = e^{j\omega}$. Bounded sequences that do not decay ($u[n]$, $\cos\omega_0 n$, $e^{j\omega_0 n}$) need Dirac impulses. $\frac{\sin(\omega_c n)}{\pi n}$ decays like $1/n$: not absolutely summable, yet its DTFT is a rectangle. Growing sequences have no DTFT.
