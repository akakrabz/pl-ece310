The $N$-point DFT is $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$, and $\langle n\rangle_N$ is $n$ modulo $N$.

{{#choices}}{{text}}{{/choices}} {{/params.statements}} The DFT evaluates the DTFT: $X[k] = X_d\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)$, bins $\frac{2\pi}{N}$ apart, and $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic. For real $x$, $X[k] = X^*[N-k]$. The inverse-DFT formula is $N$-periodic in $n$. Zero padding keeps the DTFT and only refines the grid; resolution needs more recorded samples (full-lobe separation: $\Delta\omega \gt \frac{4\pi}{N}$). An on-bin sinusoid of amplitude $A$ gives two nonzero bins of height $\frac{AN}{2}$. The Hamming window trades a twice-as-wide main lobe for much lower side lobes. Try $x[n] = \delta[n-1]$, whose DFT is $e^{-j2\pi k/N}$, or write out one sample of the inverse DFT. For a cosine, count the periods in the record (a whole number puts it on the bins). For a property, check $k = 0$ and $k = 1$ by hand, or convert the factor in front of $X[k]$ into $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}km}$ (a circular shift) or the index shift into $e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}\ell n}$ (a modulation).
