The DTFT of a finite-length signal is $X_d(\omega) = \sum_{n} x[n]\,e^{-j\omega n}$, and the $N$-point DFT of a signal of length at most $N$ (zero-padded to length $N$ if it is shorter) is $$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn},\qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1.$$

(a) A signal $x[n]$ of length at most ${{params.Na}}$ has DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ and ${{params.Na}}$-point DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{ {{params.Nam1}} }$. For which index $k$ is $X[k] = X_d\!\left({{params.wa_tex}}\right)$?

(b) A length-$4$ signal $x[n]$ with DTFT $X_d(\omega)$ is zero-padded to length $N$, and its $N$-point DFT $X[k]$ is computed. What is the smallest $N$ for which the values $X[k]$ include both $X_d\!\left({{params.wb1_tex}}\right)$ and $X_d\!\left({{params.wb2_tex}}\right)$?

(c) Let $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{ {{params.Lcm1}} } = {{params.xc_tex}}$ with ${{params.Lc}}$-point DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{ {{params.Lcm1}} }$. We zero-pad $x[n]$ with ${{params.Mz}}$ zeros to obtain $\{y[n]\}_{n=0}^{ {{params.Mm1}} }$ with ${{params.M}}$-point DFT $\{Y[k]\}_{k=0}^{ {{params.Mm1}} }$. {{#params.zp_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.zp_choices}} For $x$ of length at most $N$, $X[k] = X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{N}\right)$, $0 \le k \le N-1$: $N$ equally spaced samples of one period $[0, 2\pi)$. $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic, so a negative frequency $\omega$ is found at $\omega + 2\pi$, in the upper half of the DFT. Appending zeros adds $0\cdot e^{-j\omega n}$ terms: the DTFT does not change. The longer DFT samples the same $X_d$ on a finer grid, so the old bin $k$ sits at a new index. For a real $x$, $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$.

(a) Bin $k$ sits at $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$ in $[0, 2\pi)$. {{#params.wa_neg}}$X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic, so $X_d\!\left({{params.wa_tex}}\right) = X_d\!\left({{params.wa_2pi_tex}}\right)$.{{/params.wa_neg}} Solving $\frac{2\pi k}{ {{params.Na}} } = {{params.wa_2pi_tex}}$: $$k = {{params.ka_frac_tex}}.$$

(b) $X_d(\omega)$ is a DFT value only if $\omega = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$ for an integer $k$, i.e. $N\cdot\frac{\omega}{2\pi}$ is an integer. Here $\frac{\omega_1}{2\pi} = {{params.fb1_tex}}$ and $\frac{\omega_2}{2\pi} = {{params.fb2_tex}}$, so $N$ must be a multiple of ${{params.q1}}$ and of ${{params.q2}}$: $$N = \operatorname{lcm}({{params.q1}}, {{params.q2}}) = {{params.Nb}}\qquad(\text{bins } k = {{params.kb1}} \text{ and } k = {{params.kb2}}).$$ {{{params.trap_note_html}}}

(c) The appended zeros do not change the DTFT, so $Y[k] = X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{ {{params.M}} }\right)$ and $X[k] = X_d\!\left(\frac{2\pi k}{ {{params.Lc}} }\right)$ sample the same $X_d$; bin $a$ of $X$ is bin $a\cdot\frac{ {{params.M}} }{ {{params.Lc}} } = {{params.ratio_tex}}\,a$ of $Y$ when that is an integer{{^params.ratio_int}} (here only for even $a$: the other $X[a]$ have no partner in $Y$){{/params.ratio_int}}. Because $x$ is real, $X_d(-\omega) = X_d^*(\omega)$, so a conjugate $Y^*[b]$ is the DTFT at $-\frac{2\pi b}{ {{params.M}} }$, i.e. at $2\pi - \frac{2\pi b}{ {{params.M}} }$. (d) Zero padding never changes $X_d(\omega)$; it only samples it more densely (here ${{params.M}}$ instead of ${{params.Lc}}$ samples per period). Resolution (the main-lobe width, about $\frac{4\pi}{L}$ for $L$ samples) and leakage are fixed by the $L$ original samples, which padding does not change. It does not compress the frequency axis either: $Y_d(\omega) = X_d(\omega)$, not $X_d\!\left({{params.ratio_tex}}\,\omega\right)$. (Upsampling by an integer factor $r$, i.e. inserting $r - 1$ zeros between the samples, gives $X_d(r\omega)$; appending zeros at the end does not.)
