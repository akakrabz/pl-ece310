The $N$-point DFT of a length-$N$ signal $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ is $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}$, $k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$, and $\langle n\rangle_N$ denotes $n$ modulo $N$ (a number in $\{0, 1, \dots, N-1\}$).

(b) Find the index $k \in \{0, 1, \dots, {{params.Nm1}}\}$ for which (i) $X[k] = X_d^*\!\left({{params.wa_tex}}\right)$, and (ii) $X[k] = X_d^*\!\left({{params.wb_tex}}\right)$.

{{/params.k_fa25}} {{#params.k_fa23}} Write $\sum_{n} b^*[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn} = \Big(\sum_n b[n]\,e^{+j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}\Big)^*$ and multiply inside by $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}Nn} = e^{-j2\pi n} = 1$: the exponent becomes $-j\frac{2\pi}{N}(N-k)n$. The DFT index lives on a circle of $N$ points. $X[k] = \tfrac12\big(Z[k] + Z^*[\langle -k\rangle_N]\big)$ and $Y[k] = \tfrac{1}{2j}\big(Z[k] - Z^*[\langle -k\rangle_N]\big)$: the partner of bin $k$ is bin $N - k$, not bin $k$ (so $X[k] \ne \mathrm{Re}\{Z[k]\}$ in general), and $\frac{1}{2j} = -\frac{j}{2}$. {{/params.k_fa23}} {{#params.k_sp21}} $x[n]$ is real $\iff$ $X[k] = X^*[\langle -k\rangle_N]$ for every $k$: $X[0]$ real, $X[N/2]$ real for even $N$, and $X[k]$, $X[N-k]$ complex conjugates. The partner of $k$ is $N - k$ (circular), not $N - 1 - k$ (a plain flip of the list). The inverse DFT at $n = 0$ is $x[0] = \frac1N\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]$. A complex $x[0]$ settles the question; a real $x[0]$ proves nothing. {{/params.k_sp21}} {{#params.k_fa25}} For a signal of length $N$, $X[k] = X_d(\omega_k)$ with $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$ (here multiples of ${{params.wk_tex}}$), and $X_d$ is $2\pi$-periodic. For a real $x[n]$, $X_d^*(\omega) = X_d(-\omega)$: a conjugated DTFT value is the DTFT at the negative frequency. {{/params.k_fa25}} {{#params.k_fa23}}

(a) Conjugate the definition and multiply by $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}Nn} = e^{-j2\pi n} = 1$: $$\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b^*[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn} = \Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b[n]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}Nn}\Big)^* = \Big(\sum_{n=0}^{N-1} b[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}(N-k)n}\Big)^* = B^*[\langle N-k\rangle_N] = B^*[\langle -k\rangle_N].$$ So the answer is $\{ {{params.conj_right_tex}} \}_{k=0}^{N-1}$. (The exam writes $B^*[N-k]$; at $k = 0$ that is $B^*[N] = B^*[0]$, since the DFT is $N$-periodic in $k$.) The others: $B^*[k]$ is the DFT of $b^*[\langle -n\rangle_N]$; $B[\langle -k\rangle_N]$ is the DFT of $b[\langle -n\rangle_N]$; $B^*[N+k] = B^*[k]$ by periodicity; $-B[k]$ is the DFT of $-b[n]$; $B^*[N-1-k]$ is a plain flip of the list, not a circular one.

(b) By linearity and part (a), $X[k] = \tfrac12\big(Z[k] + Z^*[\langle -k\rangle_N]\big)$ and $Y[k] = \tfrac{1}{2j}\big(Z[k] - Z^*[\langle -k\rangle_N]\big)$. For $k = {{params.k1}}$ the partner is $\langle -{{params.k1}}\rangle_{ {{params.N}} } = {{params.k1m}}$: $Z[{{params.k1}}] = {{params.Zk1_tex}}$, $Z[{{params.k1m}}] = {{params.Zk1m_tex}}$, $Z^*[{{params.k1m}}] = {{params.Zk1mc_tex}}$. $$\begin{aligned} &{{params.X_line_tex}}, \\ &{{params.Y_line_tex}}. \end{aligned}$$ Note $X[{{params.k1}}] \ne \mathrm{Re}\{Z[{{params.k1}}]\} = {{params.ReZk1_tex}}$ and $Y[{{params.k1}}] \ne \mathrm{Im}\{Z[{{params.k1}}]\} = {{params.ImZk1_tex}}$: $X[k]$ and $Y[k]$ are complex.

(c) {{#params.k2_zero}}At $k = 0$ the partner is $\langle -0\rangle_{ {{params.N}} } = 0$ (the exam's $Z^*[N - 0] = Z^*[{{params.N}}]$ is $Z^*[0]$ by periodicity):{{/params.k2_zero}}{{#params.k2_mid}}At $k = {{params.k2}} = \frac{N}{2}$ the partner is $\langle -{{params.k2}}\rangle_{ {{params.N}} } = {{params.k2}}$, the bin itself:{{/params.k2_mid}} $$X[{{params.k2}}] = \tfrac12\left(Z[{{params.k2}}] + Z^*[{{params.k2}}]\right) = \mathrm{Re}\{Z[{{params.k2}}]\} = {{params.Xs_tex}},\qquad Y[{{params.k2}}] = \tfrac{1}{2j}\left(Z[{{params.k2}}] - Z^*[{{params.k2}}]\right) = \mathrm{Im}\{Z[{{params.k2}}]\} = {{params.Ys_tex}},$$ with $Z[{{params.k2}}] = {{params.Zk2_tex}}$. Both are real, as $X[0]$ and $X[N/2]$ of a real signal must be. Only at these self-paired bins is $X[k] = \mathrm{Re}\{Z[k]\}$.
