Ahead of the lectures: this topic is taught after Lecture 17 and appears on past Midterm 2 exams; notation follows those exams.

The $N$-point DFT of a length-$N$ signal $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ is $$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn},\qquad k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1.$$ Let $$\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{ {{params.Nm1}} } = {{params.x_tex}}$$ be a length-${{params.N}}$ signal with ${{params.N}}$-point DFT $\{X[k]\}_{k=0}^{ {{params.Nm1}} }$.

Enter exact values: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits (for example 1.414 for $\sqrt{2}$).

(a) Compute $X[0]$ and $X[{{params.half}}]$.

(b) Compute the real and imaginary parts of $X[{{params.k0}}]$.

(c) Compute the energy of the DFT values, $\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{ {{params.Nm1}} } \lvert X[k]\rvert^2$.

(d) A different length-${{params.Nv}}$ signal $\{v[n]\}_{n=0}^{ {{params.Nvm1}} }$ has the ${{params.Nv}}$-point DFT $$\{V[k]\}_{k=0}^{ {{params.Nvm1}} } = {{params.V_tex}}.$$ Find $v[0]$.

(d) A different length-$4$ signal $\{v[n]\}_{n=0}^{3}$ has the $4$-point DFT $$\{V[k]\}_{k=0}^{3} = {{params.V_tex}}.$$ Find $v[n]$ for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$, as a row vector (entries separated by commas inside brackets, e.g. [2, -1, 0]).

{{/params.inv_vec}} $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}\cdot 0\cdot n} = 1$ and $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}\frac{N}{2}n} = e^{-j\pi n} = (-1)^n$, so $X[0]$ and $X[N/2]$ are plain (alternating) sums. The inverse DFT is $x[n] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1} X[k]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}$: note the $\frac1N$ and the plus sign in the exponent; at $n = 0$ it is the average of the DFT values. For $X[k_0]$ list $e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}k_0 n} = \cos\frac{2\pi k_0 n}{N} - j\sin\frac{2\pi k_0 n}{N}$ for each $n$ (the minus sign makes the imaginary part $-\sum_n x[n]\sin\frac{2\pi k_0 n}{N}$), multiply by $x[n]$ and add. Parseval's relation for the DFT is $\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\lvert x[n]\rvert^2 = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\lvert X[k]\rvert^2$.

(a) At $k = 0$ every exponential is $1$, and at $k = {{params.half}} = \frac{N}{2}$ it is $e^{-j\pi n} = (-1)^n$: $$X[0] = \sum_{n} x[n] = {{params.X0_terms_tex}} = {{params.X0_tex}},$$ $$X[{{params.half}}] = \sum_{n} (-1)^n x[n] = {{params.XH_terms_tex}} = {{params.XH_tex}}.$$

(b) One term per sample, $X[{{params.k0}}] = \sum_{n} x[n]\,{{params.W_tex}}$:

{{{params.table_html}}}

Adding the last column: $$X[{{params.k0}}] = {{params.Xk_tex}},$$ so $\mathrm{Re}\{X[{{params.k0}}]\} = {{params.re_tex}}{{#params.re_irr}} \approx {{params.re_dec}}{{/params.re_irr}}$ and $\mathrm{Im}\{X[{{params.k0}}]\} = {{params.im_tex}}{{#params.im_irr}} \approx {{params.im_dec}}{{/params.im_irr}}$. (With $e^{+j}$ in the definition you would get the complex conjugate, with the wrong sign of the imaginary part.)

(c) Parseval's relation for the $N$-point DFT, $\sum_n \lvert x[n]\rvert^2 = \frac1N\sum_k \lvert X[k]\rvert^2$, gives without computing any other bin $$\sum_{k=0}^{ {{params.Nm1}} } \lvert X[k]\rvert^2 = N\sum_{n=0}^{ {{params.Nm1}} }\lvert x[n]\rvert^2 = {{params.N}}\left({{params.sq_terms_tex}}\right) = {{params.N}}\cdot {{params.E_tex}} = {{params.P_tex}}.$$

{{#params.inv_x0}}

(d) The inverse DFT at $n = 0$ is the average of the DFT values, $v[0] = \frac{1}{N}\sum_{k} V[k]\,e^{j\frac{2\pi}{N}k\cdot 0} = \frac1N\sum_k V[k]$. The imaginary parts cancel in the conjugate pairs $V[k]$, $V[{{params.Nv}} - k]$: $$v[0] = \frac{1}{ {{params.Nv}} }\left[{{params.Vsum_terms_tex}}\right] = \frac{ {{params.Vsum_tex}} }{ {{params.Nv}} }{{params.v0_eq_tex}}.$$

{{/params.inv_x0}} {{#params.inv_vec}}
