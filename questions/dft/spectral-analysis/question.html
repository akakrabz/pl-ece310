Ahead of the lectures: this topic is taught after Lecture 17 and appears on past Midterm 2 exams; notation follows those exams.

An analog signal $x_c(t)$ is sampled with sampling frequency $f_s = 1/T$ to give $x[n] = x_c(nT)$, and $N$ samples $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ go into the $N$-point DFT $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}$, $k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$.

Enter exact values: fractions such as -7/3 are accepted; decimals need 4 significant digits.

(a) A real signal $x_c(t)$ is sampled at $f_s = {{params.fsa_tex}}$ and $N = {{params.Na}}$ samples are used.

(i) What is the spacing between adjacent DFT samples, in hertz?

(ii) Assuming $x_c(t)$ is bandlimited below $f_s/2$ (no aliasing), which analog frequency in $\left(-\tfrac{f_s}{2}, \tfrac{f_s}{2}\right]$ does the index $k = {{params.k_hi}}$ correspond to, in hertz?

(iii) Now $x_c(t)$ is no longer assumed bandlimited below $f_s/2$. What is the smallest frequency $f \gt f_s/2$ of a cosine $\cos(2\pi f t)$ that lands exactly on bin $k = {{params.k_lo}}$ (that is, gives the same samples as a cosine at $\frac{ {{params.k_lo}}\,f_s}{N}$), in hertz?

(b) A continuous-time signal $x_c(t) = A_1\cos(\Omega_1 t + \phi_1) + A_2\cos(\Omega_2 t + \phi_2)$ with integer amplitudes $A_1, A_2 \gt 0$, unknown phases and $\Omega_1 \lt \Omega_2$ is sampled at a rate of $f_s = {{params.fsb}}$ Hz for ${{params.Db_tex}}$ {{params.Db_unit}} to produce $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{ {{params.Nbm1}} }$; there is no aliasing. Find the amplitudes and the analog frequencies (in rad/s, entered as multiples of $\pi$: type 3 for $3\pi$ rad/s).

Bin $k$ sits at the digital frequency $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}$, and sampling maps $\Omega$ to $\omega = \Omega T$, i.e. $f = \frac{\omega}{2\pi} f_s$. Bins with $k \gt \frac N2$ are negative frequencies: $\omega_k$ and $\omega_k - 2\pi$ are the same digital frequency. $A\cos(\omega_0 n + \phi) = \frac A2 e^{j\phi}e^{j\omega_0 n} + \frac A2 e^{-j\phi}e^{-j\omega_0 n}$: with a whole number of periods in the $N$ samples, each exponential puts $N\cdot\frac{A}{2}$ in one bin ($k_0$ and $N - k_0$) and nothing elsewhere. A frequency $f$ lands on the same bin as $f + m f_s$ for every integer $m$; a cosine also reaches bin $k$ through its negative-frequency half.

(a) (i) The $N$ bins split one period $f_s$ evenly: $\Delta f = \frac{f_s}{N} = \frac{ {{params.fsa}} }{ {{params.Na}} } = {{params.dfa}}$ Hz.

(ii) $k = {{params.k_hi}} \gt \frac{N}{2}$: $\omega_k = \frac{2\pi\cdot {{params.k_hi}}}{ {{params.Na}} }$ is the same digital frequency as $\omega_k - 2\pi = -\frac{2\pi\cdot {{params.Nm_hi}}}{ {{params.Na}} }$, so $f = -{{params.Nm_hi}}\cdot\Delta f = {{params.f_hi}}$ Hz (for a real signal, the mirror image of the $+{{params.f_hi_abs}}$ Hz component at $k = {{params.Nm_hi}}$).

(iii) Bin $k$ collects every $f = \frac{k f_s}{N} + m f_s$ (FA2024 #6), and a real cosine at $f$ also has a component at $-f$. Bin ${{params.k_lo}}$ is at ${{params.f_lo}}$ Hz, so it sees $\pm {{params.f_lo}}$ Hz plus multiples of $f_s$; above $\frac{f_s}{2} = {{params.fs_half}}$ Hz the smallest is $f = f_s - {{params.f_lo}} = {{params.f_alias}}$ Hz, whose negative-frequency half $-{{params.f_alias}} + {{params.fsa}} = {{params.f_lo}}$ Hz lands on bin ${{params.k_lo}}$.

(b) $N = f_s \cdot ({{params.Db_tex}}\ \text{s}) = {{params.Nb}}$ and bin $k$ is at $\Omega_k = \frac{2\pi k}{N}f_s = {{params.Om_per_bin_tex}}\,k$ rad/s. The stems at $k \gt {{params.halfN}}$ are the negative-frequency halves ($k = {{params.mk2}} = {{params.Nb}} - {{params.k2}}$ and $k = {{params.mk1}} = {{params.Nb}} - {{params.k1}}$), so there are two cosines, at $k = {{params.k1}}$ and $k = {{params.k2}}$. Each cosine puts $\frac{N A}{2} = {{params.Nb_half}}A$ in each of its two bins (the phases only change the angle of $X[k]$):

$$A_1 = \frac{ {{params.h1}} }{ {{params.Nb_half}} } = {{params.A1v}},\qquad A_2 = \frac{ {{params.h2}} }{ {{params.Nb_half}} } = {{params.A2v}},$$ $$\Omega_1 = {{params.Om_per_bin_tex}}\cdot {{params.k1}} = {{params.W1v}}\pi\ \text{rad/s}\ ({{params.f1}}\ \text{Hz}),\qquad \Omega_2 = {{params.Om_per_bin_tex}}\cdot {{params.k2}} = {{params.W2v}}\pi\ \text{rad/s}\ ({{params.f2}}\ \text{Hz}).$$
