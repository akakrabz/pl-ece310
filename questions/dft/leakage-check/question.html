Lecture 22 (DFT of a finite-length sinusoid, spectral leakage); the shapes of past Midterm 2 problems.

Throughout, the $N$-point DFT of $\{x[n]\}_{n=0}^{N-1}$ is $X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\,e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{N}kn}$, $k = 0, 1, \dots, N-1$.

At which (generally fractional) index $\kappa$ is $\frac{2\pi\kappa}{N}$ equal to the digital frequency of the cosine, and at which index $0 \le k \le {{params.Np_half}}$ does the $N$-point DFT have its greatest magnitude?

{{/params.fa21}}
For $A\cos(\omega_0 n)$ on $N$ samples, $k_0 = \frac{\omega_0 N}{2\pi}$ is the number of periods in the record. The DFT evaluates $X_d(\omega) = \frac A2 C(\omega - \omega_0) + \frac A2 C(\omega + \omega_0)$ at $\frac{2\pi k}{N}$, and $C$ is zero at every nonzero multiple of $\frac{2\pi}{N}$. $k_0$ an integer: only $X[k_0] = X[N - k_0] = \frac{AN}{2}$ are nonzero ($\omega_0 = \pi$ gives a single bin $k = \frac N2$; a sine gives $\mp j\frac{AN}{2}$). $k_0$ not an integer: (almost) every bin is nonzero and the tallest one is the bin nearest $k_0$. Zero padding keeps the DTFT and only changes where it is evaluated.

(a) A sinusoid $\cos(\omega_0 n)$ or $\sin(\omega_0 n)$ with $0 \lt \omega_0 \lt \pi$ gives exactly two nonzero DFT values when $k_0 = \frac{\omega_0 N}{2\pi}$ (the number of periods in the record) is an integer, and leaks into (almost) every bin otherwise; a sum needs this for every term.

(b) $\omega_0 = {{params.w0_tex}}$ (period ${{params.P}}$): $k_0 = \frac{\omega_0 N_1}{2\pi} = {{params.k01}}$ whole periods. The DFT evaluates both lobes at their peaks and every other bin at a zero: $$X[{{params.k01}}] = X[{{params.N1mk01}}] = \frac{A N_1}{2} = {{params.H_tex}},\qquad X[k] = 0 \text{ otherwise},$$ so $k_1 = {{params.k01}}$, $k_2 = {{params.N1}} - {{params.k01}} = {{params.N1mk01}}$ (the mirror, not $-{{params.k01}}$) and $\lvert X[k_1]\rvert = {{params.H_tex}}$.

(c) $\frac{\omega_0 N_2}{2\pi} = \frac{ {{params.N2}} }{ {{params.P}} } = {{params.k02_tex}} \approx {{params.k02_dec}}$ periods: not an integer, so the periodic extension has a jump, the DFT misses the peaks and zeros of the lobes, and the energy leaks into (almost) every bin. The largest bin is the one nearest ${{params.k02_tex}}$: $k_{\max} = {{params.kmax}}$, with $\lvert X[{{params.kmax}}]\rvert \approx {{params.peak2}}$, {{params.peak2_rel}} $\frac{A N_2}{2} = {{params.peak2_full_tex}}$ (no bin sits on the peak of the lobe).

Same shape as SP2025 MT2 #9 (DFT of $\{\cos(\frac{\pi}{2}n)\}_{n=0}^{15}$: $X[4] = X[12] = 8$) and Lecture 22 eqs. 13–14 ($\cos(\frac{\pi}{3}n)$ kept for $24$ samples: $X[4] = X[20] = 12$; for $22$ samples: $3\frac23$ periods, leakage, tallest bin $k = 4$).

(b) $x[n] = x_c\!\left(\frac{n}{ {{params.fs}} }\right)$ is a cosine at $\omega_0 = \frac{\Omega_0}{f_s} = \frac{ {{params.W0_tex}} }{ {{params.fs}} } = {{params.w0_tex}}$, i.e. $f_0 = {{params.f0_tex}}$ Hz. $\frac{2\pi k}{L} = \omega_0$ at $k = \frac{\omega_0 L}{2\pi} = f_0\cdot D = {{params.k0}}$ (a whole number of cycles), and the mirror is $L - {{params.k0}} = {{params.Lmk0}}$; every other bin is zero: $$k_1 = {{params.k0}},\quad k_2 = {{params.Lmk0}},\qquad \lvert X[k_1]\rvert = \lvert X[k_2]\rvert = \frac{A L}{2} = {{params.H_tex}}.$$

(c) Zero padding leaves the DTFT unchanged; the $N$-point DFT evaluates it at $\frac{2\pi k}{N}$, so $$\kappa = \frac{\omega_0 N}{2\pi} = \frac{f_0 N}{f_s} = \frac{ {{params.f0_tex}}\cdot {{params.Np}} }{ {{params.fs}} } = {{params.kap_tex}}\ (\approx {{params.kap_dec}}),$$ between two bins. The greatest magnitude is at the nearest bin, $k_{\max} = {{params.kmax}}$ (and its mirror ${{params.kmax_mirror}}$), with $\lvert X[{{params.kmax}}]\rvert \approx {{params.peak_pad}}$, {{params.peak_pad_rel}} $\frac{AL}{2} = {{params.H_tex}}$: no bin lands exactly on the peak of the lobe (the main lobe is $\frac{4\pi}{L}$ wide, ${{params.lobe_bins}}$ bins of the padded DFT).

Same shape as FA2021 MT2 #7 ($\cos(10\pi t)$ sampled at 100 Hz for 5 s, $L = 500$: peaks at $k = 25$ and $475$, $\lvert X\rvert = 250$; zero-padded to $1024$: $\kappa = 51.2$, peaks at $k = 51$ and $973$).
