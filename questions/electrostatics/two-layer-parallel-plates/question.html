Two infinite, parallel, perfectly conducting plates sit at $z = 0$ (grounded, $V = 0$) and at $z = z_0 = {{params.z0}}\ \mathrm{m}$ (held at $V = V_p$). The gap is filled with two slabs of perfect dielectric: $$\epsilon_1 = {{params.er1}}\,\epsilon_0\ \ (0 < z < d),\qquad \epsilon_2 = {{params.er2}}\,\epsilon_0\ \ (d < z < z_0),\qquad d = {{params.d}}\ \mathrm{m}.$$ The field in the lower slab is measured to be $\mathbf{E}_1 = {{params.E1z}}\,\hat{z}\ \mathrm{V/m}$. There is no free charge on the dielectric interface. Where a quantity is marked "$/\epsilon_0$", enter the coefficient of $\epsilon_0$ (e.g. 12 for $12\,\epsilon_0\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$). Use $\epsilon_0 = 8.854\times10^{-12}\ \mathrm{F/m}$ where a physical value is required.

$\mathbf{E}$ is purely normal to the interface, and there is no free charge there, so normal $\mathbf{D}$ is continuous: $\epsilon_1 E_{1z} = \epsilon_2 E_{2z}$. (Tangential $\mathbf{E}$ is trivially continuous — it is zero.) $V(z) = V(0) - \int_0^z E_z\,dz'$ with $V(0) = 0$; the integrand is piecewise constant, so $V$ is piecewise linear and continuous at $z = d$. For the plate charge use $\rho_s = \hat{n}\cdot\mathbf{D}$ with $\hat{n}$ pointing out of the conductor into the gap ($\hat{n} = -\hat{z}$ on the top plate). Either $C = Q/V_p$ with $Q = \rho_s(z_0) A$, or treat the two slabs as capacitors in series: $\dfrac{1}{C} = \dfrac{d}{\epsilon_1 A} + \dfrac{z_0 - d}{\epsilon_2 A}$.

(a) Normal $\mathbf{D}$ is continuous across the charge-free interface: $E_{2z} = \dfrac{\epsilon_1}{\epsilon_2}E_{1z} = \dfrac{ {{params.er1}} }{ {{params.er2}} }\times({{params.E1z}}) = {{params.E2z}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$.

(b) $V(d) = -\displaystyle\int_0^d E_{1z}\,dz = -({{params.E1z}})({{params.d}}) = {{params.Vd}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(c) $V_p = V(d) - E_{2z}(z_0 - d) = {{params.Vd}} - ({{params.E2z}})({{params.t2}}) = {{params.Vp}}\ \mathrm{V}$. The field points downward ($-\hat{z}$), from the high-potential top plate toward the grounded one.

(d) On the top plate $\hat{n} = -\hat{z}$ (out of the conductor, into the gap): $\rho_s(z_0) = -D_{2z} = -\epsilon_2 E_{2z} = {{params.rho_top}}\,\epsilon_0\ \mathrm{C/m^2}$ — positive, as expected for the plate at higher potential. (The bottom plate carries $+D_{1z} = {{params.rho_bot}}\,\epsilon_0$, equal and opposite, since $D_z$ is the same in both slabs.)

(e) $\dfrac{C}{A} = \dfrac{\rho_s(z_0)}{V_p} = \dfrac{\epsilon_0}{d/\epsilon_{r1} + (z_0-d)/\epsilon_{r2}} = {{params.C_per_A_str}}\ \mathrm{pF/m^2}$ — the series combination of the two slab capacitances.
