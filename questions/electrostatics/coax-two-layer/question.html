A coaxial Gaussian cylinder of radius $r$ and length $L$ gives $D\cdot 2\pi r L = \lambda L$ in both layers — $\mathbf{D}$ depends on free charge only. Then $\mathbf{E} = \mathbf{D}/\epsilon$ layer by layer. $V(a) - V(b) = \int_a^b E_r\,dr$, split at $r = c$. Then $\mathcal{C} = \lambda / [V(a)-V(b)]$ — a positive number in F/m. (Cross-check: the two layers are capacitors in series.)

(a) $\mathbf{D} = \hat{r}\,\dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi r}$, so $|\mathbf{D}| = {{params.D_r1_str}}\ \mathrm{nC/m^2}$ at $r = {{params.r1_mm}}$ mm. This holds in both layers.

(b) $|\mathbf{E}| = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\,\epsilon_0\,{{params.er2}}\,r} = {{params.E_r2_str}}\ \mathrm{V/m}$ at $r = {{params.r2_mm}}$ mm.

(c) $\mathbf{E} = 0$ inside the outer conductor, so a Gaussian cylinder inside its wall encloses zero net charge: $\lambda_{\text{outer}} = -\lambda = -{{params.lam_nC}}\ \mathrm{nC/m}$.

(d) $V(a)-V(b) = \dfrac{\lambda}{2\pi\epsilon_0}\left[\dfrac{\ln(c/a)}{ {{params.er1}} } + \dfrac{\ln(b/c)}{ {{params.er2}} }\right]$ with $\ln(c/a) = {{params.lnca}}$, $\ln(b/c) = {{params.lnbc}}$, giving ${{params.Vab_str}}\ \mathrm{V}$.

(e) $\mathcal{C} = \dfrac{\lambda}{V(a)-V(b)} = \dfrac{2\pi\epsilon_0}{\ln(c/a)/{{params.er1}} + \ln(b/c)/{{params.er2}} } = {{params.Cp_str}}\ \mathrm{pF/m}$ — the series combination of the two layer capacitances.
