A point charge ${{{params.q_above_tex}}}$ sits at $(0,0,+h)$ and a point charge ${{{params.q_below_tex}}}$ sits at $(0,0,-h)$, in free space. Take the normal of the entire $z = 0$ plane to be $\hat{n} = {{{params.normal_tex}}}$.

What is the electric flux $\displaystyle\psi_E = \int_{z=0}\mathbf{D}\cdot\hat{n}\,dS$ through the whole plane?

 {{#params.choices}} {{{text}}} {{/params.choices}} A point charge's field lines spread uniformly in all directions, so exactly half of them cross any infinite plane that does not contain the charge: the flux has magnitude $|q|/2$. The sign depends on whether those lines cross along or against $\hat{n}$.

With $\hat{n} = {{{params.normal_tex}}}$: the charge above the plane contributes ${{{params.contrib_above}}}$ (a positive charge above pushes lines down through the plane), the charge below contributes ${{{params.contrib_below}}}$ (a positive charge below pushes lines up through it). Total: $\psi_E = {{{params.psi_tex}}}$.

Flipping $\hat{n}$ flips every sign. A closed surface around both charges would instead give $Q_{\text{enc}} = {{{params.q_above_tex}}} {{{params.q_below_tex}}}$ regardless of geometry.
