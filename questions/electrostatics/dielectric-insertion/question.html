With the battery disconnected the plate charge cannot change, so $\rho_s$ — and hence $D_z = \rho_s$ (Gauss's law with a pillbox on the plate) — is fixed. With the battery connected the voltage, and hence $E_z = V/W$, is fixed. Whichever one is pinned, the other follows from $\mathbf{D} = \epsilon\mathbf{E}$. $\mathbf{P} = \mathbf{D} - \epsilon_0\mathbf{E} = (\epsilon_r - 1)\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}$. Energy density $w = \tfrac12\mathbf{D}\cdot\mathbf{E}$ fills the same volume before and after, so the ratio is just the ratio of $D_zE_z$. A conductivity lets charge move through the gap: $\mathbf{J} = \sigma\mathbf{E}$. Ask where that charge comes from and whether anything can replace it — the capacitor's own $RC$ circuit (Lecture 10) answers both.
