(b) Find the electrostatic potential $V(x,y,z)$ with the reference $V(0,0,0)=0$. Use the symbols x, y, z and E0; write products explicitly (e.g. 2*x**2*y).

(c) Find the volume charge density $\rho(x,y,z)$ that produces this field. Use epsilon for $\epsilon_0$.

(d) Evaluate $\displaystyle\int_A^B \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$ from $A=(0,0,0)$ to $B={{{params.B_tex}}}$ (coordinates in metres). Give the numerical coefficient of $E_0$ (i.e. your answer in units of $E_0\cdot\mathrm{m}^{ {{params.p}} }$).

Look at which coordinates each component depends on before expanding the curl determinant — most partial derivatives vanish by inspection. $dV = -\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l}$. Either spot the exact differential, or integrate along the axis-parallel path $(0,0,0)\to(x,0,0)\to(x,y,0)\to(x,y,z)$ — never antidifferentiate the three components separately and add. For a conservative field, $\int_A^B\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = V(A) - V(B)$. Mind the order.

(a) $E_x$ and $E_y$ do not depend on $z$ and $E_z$ depends only on $z$, so only the $\hat{z}$ component can be nonzero: $(\nabla\times\mathbf{E})_z = \partial_x E_y - \partial_y E_x = 0$. The field is curl-free (conservative), so a potential exists.

(b) $V = {{{params.V_tex}}}$ — check: $-\nabla V$ returns $\mathbf{E}$.

(c) $\rho = \epsilon_0\nabla\cdot\mathbf{E} = {{{params.rho_tex}}}$. Curl-free does not mean charge-free: curl and divergence answer different questions.

(d) $\int_A^B\mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = V(A)-V(B) = 0 - V(B) = {{params.lineint_val}}\,E_0$ (the potential drops along $\mathbf{E}$).
