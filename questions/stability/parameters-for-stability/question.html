{{/params.fam_single}} {{#params.fam_set}} {{#params.K_choices}}{{text}}{{/params.K_choices}} {{/params.fam_set}} Write $H(z)$ and find its poles. Causal $\Rightarrow$ ROC $\lvert z\rvert \gt \lvert p_{\max}\rvert$, so the system is stable exactly when every pole that survives cancellation is strictly inside the unit circle. If $K$ moves a pole, you get a condition on its magnitude; if $K$ only moves a zero, the zero must land on (cancel) each bad pole. A pole on the unit circle is not stable (marginal stability), unless a zero cancels it. Negative $K$ can give imaginary poles, which are fine if they are inside the circle. To place a zero at $z = p$, substitute $z^{-1} = 1/p$. A zero can also cancel a pole that $K$ moves onto it, even outside or on the unit circle.
